About

Hi, I'm Asha. I’m a multidisciplinary technologist specializing in human-machine interactions. With a Masters in Human Factors (May 2026) and a background in software engineering, I can bridge the gap between human-centered design and the tradeoffs of technical limitations. I've always been interested in people - our motivations, limitations, strengths - so I wanted to learn how to use that knowledge to build better products and systems. I transitioned to human factors because I believe my deep empathy, and love of learning and research, is an asset to the field. My favorite projects as a software engineer involved talking to users, understanding how they used a tool, learning about issues they encountered, and finding ways to solve their problem.

I was a software engineer for 10 years before taking a sabbatical for academic pursuits. I started taking classes to obtain a second bachelors degree in Psychology (I had one class left) when I was accepted into the Embry-Riddle M.S. of Human Factors program. Although Embry-Riddle specializes in aviation and transportation, the courses gave me exposure to the different disciplines and possibilities of human factors, while allowing me to gravitate my research on the topics I was most interested in. I have a genuine enthusiasm for understanding complex sociotechnical systems, and the interdependent relationship between humans and machines. I also have a strong passion for designing products that improve lives and are designed for the variability of humans.

My human factors research has concentrated on issues in health, medical devices, human-computer interaction, and wearable technology. The integration of emerging technologies in health creates interesting opportunities for how we can leverage these systems to optimize performance, while exposing the challenges to building this technology within the boundaries of human cognition.
